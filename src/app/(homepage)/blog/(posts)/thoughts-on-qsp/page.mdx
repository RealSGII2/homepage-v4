## My Backstory

I applied for my current company because I heavily aligned with its values. It is primarily a sit-down place
mixed with quick service features. My parents and I really enjoyed the customer service there; it was the primary
reason I applied.

Unfortunately, only after a few months of working there, I learned about the bad side of the business: the owner was
never there. He wasn't there to see the problems, to find solutions, or to enforce new policies.

But, he was still the owner. We came to him with criticisms. He said he'll do something. He never did. The picture
of our owner, to us, was a businessman whose word could not be trusted and was not a person you should approach for solving
your issues. Nonetheless, we were still a team who wanted to hold the business to its values and keep every customer
who approached us happy.

### The Decline

Our managers were getting fed up with the problems they had been facing for months at this point. More nuances were
added: problems arose, things changed, and the man who was there to solve them for us at the start was checked out. He
was not there to solve these new (or improved) conflicts that the business was facing.

Ideally, as managers, they would fix these issues. Unfortunately, one of the longest standing issues was that the owner
had a power trip the entire time. Sure, they were "Managers", but in practise were glorified team leads. They could send
you to break, approve a discount or refund, but not much more.

Another problem was that these "Managers" were usually the highest end of the hierarchy, immediately after us regular
staff members. With the loss of the backing of our owner and his great vision, it was up to us to think of solutions.
Nobody agreed. What became a healthy relationship between the front and back soured. A healthy relationship between
"Managers" and staff become strained.

What was once a healthy workplace became incredibly toxic, full of gossip, yelling, and stress. Nobody was happy
anymore. What used to be providing great service became a chore and a severe mental toll.

That's when one of our "Managers" stepped up to try to fit the role of General Manager. Our owner was a great
owner, but he didn't operate the business. So, a general manager would be able to see problems, fix them, and actually
enforce the policies being put out. The man who stepped up was the best fit for such a role I had seen in any company.
He *could* turn the business around.

But the owner still had his power trip. *He* was the one to fix the problems, not one of his staff members. The
general manager offer of an incredibly low $55,000 salary was turned down.

Then that manager quit.

### The Shattering

Both of his best buddies started to get fed up with the lack of support of an arguably good team member.

One submitted his two weeks. The other got so volatile that the owner, who never punishes everyone, even for
workplace violence, fired him. Unfortunately, that manager was a keystone holding the whole team together.

The day he was fired, we had four walk outs. Over the next few weeks, we ended up losing 17 employees. The lack of
support made everyone even more stressed, and the place got even more toxic. Guests stopped coming because the
atmosphere became depressing and the employees were no longer super friendly or responsive.

Yet those problems kept persisting. With the lack of people keeping them in check, they started to eat at the business.
We were now at a net loss. After 4 months, the margin of loss has only grew.

The timeline that I generally tell others:
- 5 months ago, everything was manageable.
- 4 months ago, there was a slight possibility that the business can go under.
- 3 months ago, the possibility was very real
- 2 months ago is when the few remaining managers now claim they have a "dead man's switch". They know that if they walk
out, the business will not survive for long.

## Connections to Quantum Science

Enigma has always been a great representative of the community. She's *the* icon of QS. Much like our owner. She realises
when there were problems with the group because of community backlash, finds a solution, and tells us it's going to get
better. But nothing ever changed. Just like our company.

What happened to:
- [Monthly QAC reports](https://canary.discord.com/channels/346444423271415819/945391171323183114/1247950402989588624)?
- That [Fair Budget Report](https://canary.discord.com/channels/346444423271415819/945391171323183114/1247950402989588624)?
- The [QSP banner community engagement project](https://canary.discord.com/channels/346444423271415819/945391215237533709/1301066121692315658)?
- The [Quarterly Feedback Form](https://canary.discord.com/channels/346444423271415819/945391171323183114/1395726626288828568) that we haven't seen in 4 quarters?

More problems keep stacking. They are not being removed. We've heard of the incredibly toxic work environment in SST,
but there are many complaints of the community about inactivity, inconsistency (esp. in regard to moderation), and
irrelevancy.

Several key people have already left. If tensions continue to rise, then more turnover will happen. Perhaps it will get
to a bad enough position where Enigma *will* start implementing her policies, but implementation does not work if it
cannot be enforced. Our owner has been making great strides to actually put policy into writing, but he isn't there to
make sure people are following the new rules, and our floor leaders have already lost faith in him. They only work on
their own accord.

## My Honest Takes

Obviously, the toxic environment in SST is a major problem right now. Several people I know have expressed major doubts
about QSOC as an actual entity—lack of standardisation and activity leading to confusing punishments and spotty moderation.

Our company's managers have wildly varying takes on how many different tasks should be handled.

As much as I disagree with many choices others are making, they have no support system. There is nobody to bring up
(or force others to listen to) other viewpoints. Nobody to make others accountable. Every department is running themselves
with no [clear vision](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1AuY6-EI_JnkJHXOLkUVTEiFfx265V6yKZOmy9k5XI2g/edit?tab=t.0) and
it falls apart within themselves and a network of departments running together.

I am currently working with high school friends. They were great to talk to in school, and are great outside of work, but
I've seen their ugliest side working with them. They are better than that, but the stress and ego of having to manage
everything yourself without the tools or knowledge to do so wears *everyone* down. I'm also a victim of this.

### Can It Be Fixed?

Yes. My business would be fixed this way, and QS would benefit well from this.

Enigma is undeniably a great owner. The *icon* of QS we know her as. She has a great vision, wants the best for the group, and
has great ideas to bring the group forward and out of the downward spiral it is in. The fact she has calmed down several
waves of a large, upset group with just sending many words shows this.

The lack of actual action and enforcement is where she falls. Words only temporarily bandage a situation, but doesn't actually
repair what caused that situation. Two hours per week won't make the cut either.

We need an [operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer). One to actually follow through with the changes Enigma wants. They would have to have major administrative power,
because if restructuring were to happen, they'd need to be able to do that. This person would uphold Enigma's vision and
keep everyone running through that same vision. Everyone would be held accountable, and actually be *guided* to solve all
of their issues instead of running around like a headless chicken.

Sure, it involves Enigma's 'operating power' being challenged. She can still own the group. She can still be the icon.
If she can't be the [owner *and* operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owner-operator), then she should hire the operator.
And if they don't perform to expectations? She still owns the group, she can fire them. And it'll be a welcome change, because
with an unhappy community, the first thing they'd like to see is a change in a forward direction.
